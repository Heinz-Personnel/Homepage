Recognition Procedure for International Nursing Professionals: The Process Explained

A suitable nursing professional has been found, yet it often takes months before they can actually start work. What state recognition means for your workforce planning, and how professionals can already start working while it's underway.

Heinz Personnel Solutions · In-Depth Article

A suitable nursing professional has been found. Yet it often takes months before they can actually start work. The reason: the nursing profession is a regulated one in Germany. Anyone who wants to work as a qualified nurse needs state authorization. This article explains what that means for your planning.

Who reviews the recognition?

The responsible body is the state authority of the respective federal state, usually the regional office or state administrative office. It compares the training completed abroad with the German framework curriculum for nursing professionals and checks its equivalence.

The language proof: B1 for entry, B2 for the job

Depending on the exact procedure, a B1 language certificate is theoretically enough for entry into Germany. In practice, Heinz consistently insists on B2 anyway, not because it's required for entry, but because everyday nursing work demands it: conversations with patients, handovers within the team, documentation. Anyone starting at B1 can quickly hit their limits in daily practice, for the professional as much as for the organization.

For full recognition, B2 is mandatory regardless. Since 1 January 2024, this can also be demonstrated through the B2 Pflege specialist language exam, an exam tailored specifically to everyday nursing work: a simulated patient conversation, a conversation among colleagues, and a typical piece of writing such as a care report.

The paths to full recognition

If the foreign training is not fully recognized as equivalent, the nursing professional receives a deficiency notice. Closing that gap isn't a dead end, it's a regulated process with more than one option:

  1. Adaptation course: practical training directly at the organization, accompanied by a vocational school, usually up to six months.
  2. Knowledge exam: a state exam at a nursing vocational school for professionals from third countries, with an oral and a practical component.
  3. Aptitude exam: the equivalent procedure for professionals with an EU/EEA qualification.

For professionals from third countries, the usual case at Heinz, there is generally a right to choose between the adaptation course and the knowledge exam. Which path fits depends on individual prior training and local capacity, so we assess this for every professional individually.

Entry despite a deficiency notice: already working while recognition is underway

A deficiency notice doesn't automatically mean waiting abroad. Since March 2024, the so-called Anerkennungspartnerschaft (recognition partnership) has made it possible to enter Germany even before recognition is complete. The condition is a binding agreement between the professional and the organization to continue the procedure promptly, in Germany.

During this transition phase, the professional is already working, usually as a nursing auxiliary, as an adaptation trainee, or as a nursing professional under recognition, depending on the size of the identified gap. Always under professional supervision, but not sitting on the sidelines. For the organization, that means an additional, working pair of hands on the floor, before the procedure is even finished.

From our own experience, this transition phase, depending on the specific deficiency notice, takes between three and up to twelve months. We keep an eye on the process and actively push back when a procedure risks dragging on longer than necessary.

How long does it really take?

It's worth being honest here: the overall procedure generally takes several months, depending on the federal state and how complete the submitted documents are. Places for adaptation courses are in demand and sometimes limited. This is a real hurdle in workforce planning, not a formality.

That is exactly why careful preparation from the very start makes the decisive difference.
