Apprentices from Abroad: The Legal Rules in Germany

More and more employers in Germany train young people from outside the EU. Which rules apply to visa, language and pay, what the Federal Employment Agency checks and what happens after graduation.

Heinz Personnel Solutions · In-Depth Article · As of October 2026

Many employers can no longer fill their apprenticeship places. At the same time, young people from Asia want to train in Germany. To make it work, they need a visa for vocational training. The good news: the rules are clear, and the placement agency takes care of most of the steps. This article shows what matters for your company.

The basis: the vocational training visa

Anyone from outside the EU who comes to Germany for in-company vocational training needs a residence permit under Section 16a of the Residence Act. The requirement is a signed training contract with a company in Germany, in a state-recognised training occupation. The permit is valid for the duration of the training.

German: B1 is required, B2 is our standard

For the training visa, German at B1 level is usually required. At Heinz, apprentices arrive with B2, proven before entry. The reason is practical: vocational school classes are held in German, technical terms included. With B2, the start at work and at school is much easier.

The training allowance must cover living costs

For the visa, the apprentice's living costs must be secured. In company-based training, the allowance counts as proof if it amounts to at least €1,048 gross or €822 net per month in 2026. The statutory minimum allowance in the first year, €724, is below that. If the allowance is lower, the gap can be covered by a blocked account or a formal obligation (Verpflichtungserklärung).

In nursing, this is rarely an issue: under the collective agreement for apprentices in the public sector (TVAöD Pflege), the first-year allowance has been around €1,490 gross since May 2026. In other trades it is worth checking before the start. We clarify this with you in the first conversation.

Approval by the Federal Employment Agency

For in-company training, the Federal Employment Agency must give its approval. It mainly checks that your apprentices from abroad are hired on the same terms as German apprentices: pay, working hours and holidays. The procedure runs internally between the authorities. For your company, this means fair, comparable conditions from day one.

School leaving certificate: nursing follows the Nursing Professions Act

The required school certificate depends on the occupation. For training as a registered nurse (Pflegefachfrau or Pflegefachmann), Section 11 of the Nursing Professions Act requires an intermediate school certificate or ten years of general schooling. The foreign certificate is assessed by the responsible state authority. Heinz recommends twelve years of school to applicants. This is not mandatory, but it makes vocational school easier. For dual training in trades or logistics, the company usually decides.

Faster visa: the fast-track procedure for skilled workers

Training companies can apply for the fast-track procedure under Section 81a of the Residence Act at the responsible immigration office. The office bundles the checks in advance, and the embassy grants an appointment faster. There is a fee, but it often saves weeks. We discuss together whether it makes sense in your case.

During the training

  • The residence permit covers the entire training period.
  • A side job of up to 20 hours per week is allowed if it is independent of the training.
  • A preparatory German course in Germany is possible if needed.

After graduation: your skilled worker stays

With a German qualification, there is no recognition procedure for a foreign qualification. Anyone who continues working with you switches to a residence permit as a skilled worker without having to leave Germany. Anyone still looking for a job may stay for up to 18 months to find one. After two years of employment, a permanent settlement permit is possible if the other requirements are met.

For your company, this means: someone you trained knows your processes and has a real reason to stay.

Checklist for your company

  1. Authorisation to train. Most companies already have it. If not, your chamber can help.
  2. Training contract. On the same terms as for German apprentices.
  3. Check the allowance. At least €1,048 gross per month, or a plan for the gap.
  4. Timeline. Expect around 3 to 6 months from selection to the start of training.

Frequently asked questions

Which visa do apprentices from abroad need?
A residence permit for vocational training under Section 16a of the Residence Act, based on a training contract with your company.

What level of German is required?
Usually B1. Heinz aims for B2 because vocational school classes are in German.

How high must the training allowance be?
In 2026 at least €1,048 gross per month to count as proof of living costs. A gap can be covered by a blocked account or a formal obligation.

Can apprentices stay after graduating?
Yes. They can continue working as skilled workers or look for a job for up to 18 months.
